1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Citizens in India frequently have legitimate rights and entitlements — the
right to information (RTI), tenant protections, consumer protections, and
workplace rights — that go unused. This is not because the rights don't
exist, but because:

  - Legal and bureaucratic language is dense, intimidating, and inaccessible
    to a non-lawyer.
  - Relevant information is scattered across acts, PDFs, government portals,
    and notices that are not written to solve one person's specific problem.
  - Even when a citizen knows they have a right, they don't know:
      (a) which law/section applies to their situation,
      (b) which authority/department to approach, and
      (c) how to phrase and format a formal request correctly.
  - The friction of drafting a correctly formatted application (e.g. an RTI)
    or understanding dispute-resolution steps (e.g. a tenant deposit dispute)
    causes most people to simply give up.

The result: a large gap between "rights that exist on paper" and "rights
that are actually exercised."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. SOLUTION OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We are building a focused, two-feature AI web application that converts a
citizen's plain-language problem into two concrete forms of help:

  1. RTI Drafting Agent
     Converts a plain-language question ("I want to know why my road hasn't
     been repaired in 6 months") into a properly formatted, submission-ready
     RTI application addressed to the correct public authority/department,
     citing the relevant provisions of the RTI Act, 2005.

  2. Rights Navigator
     Takes a plain-language description of a tenant, consumer, or workplace
     dispute ("my landlord is refusing to return my security deposit") and
     explains, in simple language, what legal rights apply and what concrete
     steps the person can take — grounded in the relevant law, not generic
     LLM guesswork.

Both features share one core design principle for credibility: answers are
grounded in real source text (the RTI Act / relevant tenant-rights statute)
via retrieval, and every explanation is tied back to a citable section,
rather than being an unconstrained LLM free-text answer. This directly
addresses the biggest risk in this problem category: confidently wrong
legal information.

Scope note: This prototype intentionally excludes Scheme Eligibility Reader
and Conversational Form-Filler (also listed as illustrative directions in
the problem statement) to keep the build achievable within the ~4-5 day
hackathon window. Depth on two features beats shallow coverage of four.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. TARGET USER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - A citizen (any literacy/legal background) who has a grievance against a
    government department, landlord, seller/service provider, or employer,
    and does not know the correct legal process or wording to act on it.
  - Primary demo persona: an urban/semi-urban Indian citizen navigating a
    tenant deposit dispute or seeking government information via RTI.
  - Language: English + Hindi (Hindi as a stretch goal, not committed scope
    unless time allows after core flow is stable).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. GOALS AND SUCCESS METRICS (for the hackathon demo)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary goal: a judge can input a real-world plain-language scenario live,
on stage, and get a correct, well-formatted, source-grounded output within
seconds — with no crashes, no hallucinated law citations, and no dead ends.

Demo success criteria:
  - RTI flow: user query -> generated RTI application (correct format,
    correct department field, cites RTI Act provisions) -> downloadable
    as PDF/text, in under 15 seconds.
  - Rights Navigator flow: user query -> plain-language explanation of
    rights + numbered actionable next steps -> citation to the specific
    law/section, in under 15 seconds.
  - Zero fabricated legal citations in the demo path (tested repeatedly
    before submission).
  - Judges can change the input mid-demo and still get a sensible, distinct
    output (proves it's not a hardcoded/scripted response).

Non-goals for this prototype:
  - Not a substitute for professional legal advice (explicitly disclaimed
    in the UI).
  - Not covering every Indian state's tenant law — pick ONE state/act to
    ground Rights Navigator in for the prototype (e.g. Model Tenancy Act,
    2021, plus consumer protection basics under Consumer Protection Act,
    2019) and be transparent about that scope in the UI and README.
  - Not building user accounts, payment, or admin dashboards.

5. FEATURES — DETAILED REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 FEATURE 1: RTI DRAFTING AGENT
----------------------------------
User story:
  "As a citizen, I want to describe my information request in plain
  language and receive a properly formatted RTI application addressed to
  the right department, so that I don't need to understand legal drafting
  or bureaucratic structure myself."

Inputs:
  - Free-text plain-language question/grievance (required)
  - State/region (optional dropdown, to help route to correct authority)
  - User's name, address, contact (form fields, required for the final doc)

Processing pipeline:
  1. Parse the plain-language input to extract: subject matter, likely
     government department/ministry, and specific information being sought.
  2. Retrieve relevant RTI Act, 2005 provisions (e.g. Section 6 — request
     procedure, Section 7 — response timelines, fee provisions) from a
     local knowledge base (not live web scraping — pre-indexed for speed
     and reliability during the demo).
  3. Map the subject matter to a public authority using a small curated
     lookup table (e.g. "road repair" -> Municipal Corporation / PWD;
     "ration card" -> Food & Civil Supplies Dept). This lookup table is
     intentionally hand-curated and limited in scope for the prototype —
     documented clearly as a known limitation, not hidden.
  4. Generate the RTI application using a fixed, correctly formatted
     template (To: [Public Information Officer, Department], Subject,
     Body citing Section 6(1), applicant details, date).
  5. Render as downloadable PDF (or plain text if time-constrained) and
     display for review before download.

Output requirements:
  - Correctly structured RTI application text (matches real-world RTI
     format conventions).
  - Visible citation of the RTI Act section(s) used.
  - A clear, non-hidden note: "Please verify the department address before
    submission. This is a drafting aid, not a legal filing service."

Explicitly out of scope for this feature (state clearly in README/demo):
  - Auto-submission of the RTI to any government portal.
  - Full nationwide department address database (use a small demo dataset
    covering a handful of common categories, documented as such).
  - Tracking RTI response status.


5.2 FEATURE 2: RIGHTS NAVIGATOR
---------------------------------
User story:
  "As a citizen facing a tenant, consumer, or workplace dispute, I want to
  describe my situation in plain language and understand what my rights
  are and what steps to take, so I can act instead of feeling stuck."

Inputs:
  - Free-text description of the dispute (required)
  - Dispute category (optional selector: Tenant / Consumer / Workplace —
    can also be auto-classified from the free text)

Processing pipeline:
  1. Classify the dispute into one of the three supported categories
     (tenant / consumer / workplace) using either a lightweight classifier
     prompt or keyword-based routing (keyword routing is faster to build
     and more reliable for a live demo — recommended given the timeline).
  2. Retrieve relevant grounding text from a pre-indexed knowledge base
     covering ONE clearly scoped legal source per category, e.g.:
       - Tenant: Model Tenancy Act, 2021 (security deposit, eviction
         notice period, repair obligations)
       - Consumer: Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (right to file complaint,
         consumer forums, timelines)
       - Workplace: Payment of Wages Act / Industrial Disputes Act basics
         (scope this down further if time is short — even covering just
         wage-payment disputes is a legitimate, demoable slice)
  3. Generate a plain-language explanation with:
       - What the law says relevant to this specific situation
       - Numbered, concrete next steps (e.g. "1. Send a written notice to
         your landlord requesting deposit return within 7 days. 2. If no
         response, file a complaint with the Rent Authority under Section
         X...")
       - Citation of the specific act/section used
  4. Display disclaimer: "This explains general legal rights based on
     [Act Name]. It is not a substitute for professional legal advice."

Output requirements:
  - Plain-language explanation (no legalese repeated verbatim).
  - Actionable, numbered steps — not just an explanation.
  - Visible source citation for every substantive claim.

Explicitly out of scope for this feature:
  - Multi-state legal variation (tenant law varies significantly by state
    in India — prototype uses ONE reference act and states this clearly).
  - Predicting case outcomes or providing a legal opinion.
  - Connecting the user to an actual lawyer or filing on their behalf.

6. SHARED SYSTEM REQUIREMENT: GROUNDING & ANTI-HALLUCINATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both features MUST use retrieval-augmented generation against a small,
pre-indexed, hand-picked set of real legal source documents rather than
relying purely on an LLM's parametric knowledge. This is the single most
important non-negotiable requirement in this PRD, because:
  - The problem statement is legal/civic in nature — factual accuracy is
    the core value proposition, not a nice-to-have.
  - Judges are highly likely to test edge cases live; an ungrounded LLM
    answer that "sounds right" but cites a nonexistent section will be
    immediately damaging.

Implementation approach (kept simple for the timeline):
  - Manually collect and clean 2-4 short source documents (RTI Act key
    sections, Model Tenancy Act key sections, Consumer Protection Act
    key sections). Do NOT try to ingest full acts — extract only the
    provisions relevant to the demo scenarios.
  - Chunk and embed these documents (a simple vector store is sufficient
    — e.g. FAISS or Chroma running locally in the Python service).
  - Retrieve top-matching chunks per query and pass them into the LLM
    prompt as grounding context, instructing the model to cite only from
    provided context and to state uncertainty if the query falls outside
    the provided source material.

7. TECH STACK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Frontend:
  - React (Create React App or Vite) — single-page app, two main flows
    (RTI Drafting Agent, Rights Navigator) accessible from a simple nav.
  - Plain CSS or a lightweight component library (avoid heavy design
    systems given the time budget) — clean over flashy.

Backend (application layer):
  - Node.js + Express — handles routing, request validation, user-facing
    API endpoints, and serves the frontend build.
  - MongoDB — stores: department lookup table, generated document history
    (optional, for demo continuity), and any static reference data.

AI / Retrieval layer:
  - Python microservice (FastAPI or Flask), called by the Express backend
    via internal REST API.
  - LLM: any available API-based model (e.g. via Anthropic/OpenAI API) for
    generation — do NOT attempt to self-host a model given the timeline.
  - Retrieval: a simple vector store (FAISS/Chroma) with pre-embedded
    source documents (RTI Act excerpts, Model Tenancy Act excerpts,
    Consumer Protection Act excerpts).
  - PDF generation: a lightweight library (e.g. reportlab in Python, or
    a Node PDF library) to render the RTI application as a downloadable
    document.

Dev/build tools:
  - Antigravity / Codex (AI coding assistants) used for scaffolding routes,
    boilerplate CRUD, and repetitive UI components — NOT relied upon for
    scoping decisions or for the retrieval/grounding logic, which needs
    careful manual verification.

Deployment (for the "live/hosted" submission requirement):
  - Frontend: Vercel or Netlify (fast, free, simple).
  - Backend + Python service: Render or Railway (free tier sufficient
    for demo traffic).
  - If hosting proves time-consuming, fall back to clear local-run
    instructions in the README, as explicitly permitted by the hackathon
    rules.

10. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Risk: LLM hallucinates a legal citation or department name.
  Mitigation: Strict RAG grounding (Section 6); test the exact demo
  scenarios repeatedly before submission; instruct model explicitly to
  say "not covered by available sources" rather than guess.

  Risk: Scope creep — trying to cover too many states/laws/categories.
  Mitigation: Hard-lock scope to ONE act per category, documented in
  README as a known/intentional limitation, not hidden.

  Risk: Live demo failure (hosting downtime, API rate limits).
  Mitigation: Have a local fallback ready; record a backup screen capture
  of a successful run in case live demo fails during Phase 1 video or
  finale presentation.

  Risk: Time lost on infrastructure (auth, deployment, styling) instead
  of core AI logic.
  Mitigation: No user accounts/auth for the prototype; minimal styling;
  prioritize the retrieval + generation pipeline first, polish last.
